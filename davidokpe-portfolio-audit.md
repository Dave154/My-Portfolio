# David Okpe Portfolio Audit & Improvement Plan

**Site reviewed:** `davidokpe.cv` / `www.davidokpe.cv`  
**Review date:** September 21, 2026  
**Primary goals:**
1. Reposition the portfolio around David as a full-stack developer who solves business problems.
2. Make the site more useful to founders, businesses, and potential clients.
3. Improve the site's ability to establish **David Okpe** as the person/entity behind the site.
4. Improve the technical and on-page SEO foundations so the portfolio can be discovered and indexed for searches such as **David Okpe**, **David Okpe developer**, and related professional searches.
5. Keep the existing visual identity and overall design direction unless a change is necessary for clarity, usability, SEO, or conversion.

---

# 1. Executive Summary

The current portfolio looks polished and technically impressive, but its biggest weakness is not the visual design.

The main problem is **what the site tells a stranger about David**.

Right now, the site leads with:

> NOT JUST  
> SIDE PROJECTS.

followed by:

> Building software that actually scales. Specializing in complex commerce, multi-tenant architecture, and intuitive design. Currently battle-tested at JelloSite.

That positions David primarily as a technically sophisticated developer.

The desired positioning is broader and more useful to the people we want the portfolio to convince:

> **Full-Stack Developer | I turn business problems into working software.**

The portfolio should communicate that same idea.

A founder should quickly understand:

- who David is
- what kind of problems he solves
- what he builds
- what real systems he has worked on
- what his role was
- how to contact him

A search engine should also be able to clearly understand:

- this site belongs to **David Okpe**
- David is a **full-stack developer**
- he is associated with the LinkedIn profile
- he works with web applications, business systems, e-commerce, APIs, databases, and product development
- the projects and experience described on the site belong to the same person

The current site has good raw material. The main job is to make the identity, positioning, content hierarchy, and search signals much clearer.

# 3. Highest-Priority Problems

## P0 — Identity is too weak for a personal portfolio

The site uses **Dave** prominently:

> Dave — 2026

and:

> © 2026 DAVE.

But the name we want people to search for and associate with the portfolio is **David Okpe**.

This matters even more because there are multiple people named David Okpe on LinkedIn and elsewhere.

The current LinkedIn profile we want associated with this site is:

**David Okpe — Lagos — Darllix — 2K followers**

The portfolio needs to make the relationship between:

**David Okpe → portfolio → LinkedIn → GitHub → projects**

obvious.

### Fix

Use **David Okpe** as the primary name everywhere on the website.

Examples:

- Page title: `David Okpe | Full-Stack Developer`
- Visible name: `David Okpe`
- Footer: `© 2026 David Okpe. All rights reserved.`
- About/record section: explicitly say `I'm David Okpe...`
- Structured data: `Person.name = David Okpe`
- `sameAs`: LinkedIn, GitHub, and other legitimate profiles

Do not rely on `Dave` as the main identity signal.

# 4. P0 — The homepage title is poor for Google

The crawled page currently exposes:

> `Dave — 2026`

as the document title.

That is one of the clearest problems on the entire site.

Google recommends descriptive, concise page titles and specifically warns against vague titles. Google also uses the page title, prominent headings, page content, links, and other signals when generating search result title links.

### Replace with

Recommended:

> **David Okpe | Full-Stack Developer**

Alternative:

> **David Okpe | Full-Stack Developer & Software Engineer**

The first option is cleaner and matches the positioning we are building.

Do not use:

- Dave — 2026
- Portfolio
- Home
- David Okpe Portfolio 2026 Full Stack Developer React Next Node JavaScript TypeScript

Avoid keyword stuffing.

# 41. Google Search Console should be part of the launch process

After the site changes:

1. Add the site to Google Search Console.
2. Verify ownership.
3. Inspect the homepage URL.
4. Check whether Google can access the page.
5. Check indexing status.
6. Submit `sitemap.xml`.
7. Request indexing for the homepage.
8. Inspect important project pages after publishing them.
9. Monitor search queries and impressions.
10. Fix crawl/indexing issues reported by Search Console.

Google explicitly recommends Search Console for monitoring how Google sees a site and recommends URL Inspection after structured-data/page changes.

Do not assume that deploying the site means Google has indexed it.

# 56. Important Search Reality

The goal is **not** to guarantee a first-place Google result for `David Okpe`.

No on-page change can guarantee that.

Google states that meeting its technical requirements and best practices does not guarantee that a page will be crawled, indexed, or served in search results.

The realistic goal is to give Google strong, consistent evidence that:

**David Okpe → this website → this professional profile → this work**

is one entity, while making the website genuinely useful to people who land on it.

That is the foundation we should build first.
